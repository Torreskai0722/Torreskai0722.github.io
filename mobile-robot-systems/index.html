Why take this course

A policy that is brilliant in a notebook is useless on a robot if it can't meet the latency budget — and dangerous if it fails silently. This course lives on the seam where deployed autonomy actually succeeds or fails.

Real hardware

End on a real robot

Every project develops sim-first, then deploys to Jetson-powered ground robots or Crazyflie micro-drones and measures the sim-to-real gap. "Does it actually work?" becomes a number.

Two-way bridge

ML or systems — not both

The course levels each side up on the other: the modular autonomy pipeline (sensing, perception, planning, control) for systems folks; real-time, edge, and inference systems for ML folks.

Project-driven

Build, measure, publish

No final exam. The capstone is a semester project — make an autonomy capability efficient, then defend it with measurements in a workshop-style paper and talk.

What you'll build

Eight project directions — every one touches both sides of the bridge and ends with a hardware reality-check. Or propose your own.

Anytime perception on a budget

An early-exit / cascaded detector; map the accuracy–latency–energy frontier.

Visuomotor policy on a deadline

A sim-trained driving policy quantized and accelerated until it hits the control deadline on-device; task success vs. latency, sim vs. real.

Adaptive edge offloading

Split perception between robot and edge under replayed network traces; the split adapts as the link degrades.

LiDAR localization on a memory budget

2-D SLAM with a bounded-memory map; the accuracy vs. memory/latency frontier; real drift vs. sim.

Compress a big model for the edge

Distill / quantize a large vision model onto the Orin Nano within a frame-time budget; measure the accuracy cost.

Sim-to-real warmup study

How a short, cheap on-robot adaptation phase closes the sim-to-real gap — the reality-check is the core of the project.

Collaborative perception

Two robots — or a drone swarm — share detections over WiFi; accuracy gain vs. bandwidth and latency cost.

Autonomy-stack measurement study

Profile a ROS 2 pipeline on the Orin Nano under load; locate the efficiency bottlenecks.

Three labs, one ladder

Each lab builds a skill on one side of the bridge — the last crosses it on real hardware.

Run a learned autonomy component

Train or deploy a perception or control component in simulation and characterize its task performance. Out Sep 3 · due Sep 22.

Profile it as a system

Put it in a ROS 2 graph, instrument it, and measure end-to-end latency and where time actually goes. Out Sep 29 · due Oct 13.

Cross the bridge on real hardware

Deploy to a Jetson robot: compress and accelerate until it hits an on-device latency budget — measure accuracy, latency, and energy on the device. Out Oct 22 · due Nov 12.

The fleet

🚗 Jetson ground robots ×4

JetBot-class differential drive: NVIDIA Orin Nano + camera + 2-D LiDAR. The on-device efficiency platform — compression, anytime inference, and localization projects run here.

🚁 Crazyflie drones ×3

27-gram open-source micro-quadcopters — too small to compute onboard, so they embody the offloading problem, have a sharper sim-to-real gap, and are swarm-native.

Development is sim-first (Gazebo / Isaac Sim, public datasets, cloud GPUs); the fleet is booked late-term for every project's hardware reality-check.
